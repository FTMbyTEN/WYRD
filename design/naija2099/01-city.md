# NAIJA 2099 — Part 1: The City

> Lagos, seventy-five years on. Still loud, still generous, still hustling — now with sea walls, solar skins, drone lanes and a city mind called WYRD. Futuristic, but you would know it was Lagos with your eyes closed.

The playable map is built on the real OpenStreetMap streets of Lagos (every road, footprint and shoreline sits where it really is). Districts below are anchored to real places; "2099" changes what stands on them, not where they are.

**Design rule for every district:** the future is *layered on top of* what Lagos already is — never replacing it. A 2099 market still haggles; a maglev still has a conductor shouting the route.

---

## 1. Districts

Each district lists: Architecture · Economy · Culture · Transport · NPC routines · Major conflict · Visual identity · Local slang · Businesses · Gameplay.

### 1.1 Èkó Island (Lagos Island, Marina, Broad Street, Balogun, Idumota)
- **Architecture:** Brazilian-quarter shophouses restored with solar-tile roofs beside 1970s banking towers re-skinned in green glass; the Cathedral Church of Christ and Central Mosque still anchor the skyline. Arcaded walkways over flood channels.
- **Economy:** Wholesale trade (Balogun, Idumota), old money (banks on Broad Street), fabric, electronics repair, informal currency exchange.
- **Culture:** Ẹ̀yọ̀ (Adamu Orisha) festival processions, Eid prayers on the Marina, Sunday church crowds, the oldest families of Lagos and their land disputes.
- **Transport:** Danfo terminus at Obalende, ferries from the Marina jetty, the Carter and Eko bridges, a maglev station under the old CMS bus stop.
- **NPC routines:** Traders open shutters at 6:30, the "load-carriers" (alábàárù) move goods until noon, bankers leave at 5, night markets light up after 7, Friday Jumat empties the streets at 1pm.
- **Major conflict:** *Who owns the island?* Old Lagos families vs. the Atlantic Rise development consortium buying up flood-damaged shophouses.
- **Visual identity:** Ochre walls, white arches, a thousand market umbrellas from above, gold light at dusk off the lagoon.
- **Slang:** "Ọjà ti kún" (the market is full — i.e. too busy), "Island people no dey cross bridge for nothing," "Na Èkó we dey, no shaking."
- **Businesses:** Fabric stalls, phone-repair stalls, bureau de change, bukka (Iya Basira's), a pharmacy run by a retired WYRD engineer.
- **Gameplay:** Deliveries through dense markets, land-title investigations, festival logistics, market price speculation, the island flood crisis questline.

### 1.2 Ikoyi & Obalende
- **Architecture:** Colonial-era villas behind high walls, white residential towers, rewilded mangrove edges by Five Cowries Creek.
- **Economy:** Diplomacy, private clinics, wealth management, domestic work (the people who keep Ikoyi running live in Obalende).
- **Culture:** Polo club, galleries, embassy parties — and Obalende's suya spots, motor parks and night buka.
- **Transport:** Private hover-cars, Falomo bridge, Obalende motor park (the city's busiest danfo hub).
- **NPC routines:** Drivers wait outside villas all morning; maids and gatemen commute at 5am from the mainland; Obalende wakes at 4am.
- **Conflict:** Gated privilege vs. the workers who serve it; drone-security surveillance creep.
- **Visual:** Clipped lawns and white towers above; dust, kiosks and danfos below the flyover.
- **Slang:** "Big man/big madam," "Oga at the top," "Na Ikoyi money, e no dey finish."
- **Businesses:** Private security firm, art gallery, motor-park union office, suya stand.
- **Gameplay:** Chauffeur and security careers, social climbing, labour organising, burglary (with heavy consequences).

### 1.3 Victoria Island & Eko Atlantic
- **Architecture:** Glass corporate towers (Civic Centre, the old Eko Hotel block), and on Eko Atlantic — reclaimed land behind the Great Wall of Lagos — gleaming supertalls, private canals, desalination plants.
- **Economy:** Finance, tech HQs, luxury retail, the Lagos Stock Exchange's AI trading floor, wedding planning, nightlife.
- **Culture:** Owambe weddings with drone-light shows, Afrobeats concert venues, rooftop lounges, Bar Beach memories.
- **Transport:** Autonomous hover-cars, water taxis on Eko Atlantic's canals, the Lekki-Ikoyi link bridge, a premium maglev line.
- **NPC routines:** 7am traffic of autonomous cars; lunchtime suya queue; club valets at midnight; sea-wall maintenance crews at 3am.
- **Conflict:** Eko Atlantic's sea wall pushes storm surges onto poorer neighbouring coasts; who pays for climate defence?
- **Visual:** Blue glass, white plazas, palm boulevards — the look of the poster — and neon by night.
- **Slang:** "VI no be for small pikin" (VI is not for the faint-hearted), "Shayo" (drinking), "Na soft life."
- **Businesses:** Event planners, tech start-ups, luxury car rental, sea-wall engineering co-op.
- **Gameplay:** Corporate espionage, event production, influencer economy, sea-wall emergencies, high-stakes negotiation.

### 1.4 Lekki Peninsula (Lekki Phase 1 to Ajah and the Toll Gate)
- **Architecture:** Estates of duplexes, half-finished buildings that become squatter communities, beach clubs, the toll gate plaza now a memorial and transit hub.
- **Economy:** Real estate, beach tourism, logistics warehouses, small manufacturing, remote work.
- **Culture:** The #EndSARS memorial at the toll gate (handled respectfully: a quiet place of remembrance, never a game backdrop for violence), beach parties, estate WhatsApp politics.
- **Transport:** The Lekki-Epe expressway (often flooded), BRT bus lanes, okada lanes, coastal ferries.
- **NPC routines:** Estate residents jog at dawn, beach vendors set up by 10, Sunday traffic to church and to the beach.
- **Conflict:** Coastal erosion vs. estate expansion; private estate "governments" vs. public access to beaches.
- **Visual:** Pastel duplexes, unfinished concrete frames, sand drifting on roads, ocean always visible.
- **Slang:** "Estate chairman," "Na this Lekki traffic go kill person," "Japa" (emigrate).
- **Businesses:** Estate security, beach bar, solar installer, logistics depot.
- **Gameplay:** Property flipping, estate politics, flood rescue, beach-business management.

### 1.5 Yaba Tech Corridor (Yaba, Sabo, Akoka, UNILAG)
- **Architecture:** Converted warehouses with server farms, student hostels, Tejuosho market rebuilt as a vertical mall, murals everywhere.
- **Economy:** Software, robotics, gaming studios, repair culture, student labour, crypto and its scams.
- **Culture:** Hackathons, campus politics, Afrobeats producers in bedroom studios, spoken word nights.
- **Transport:** Third Mainland approach, okada and e-bike swarms, the Yaba rail station.
- **NPC routines:** Students up at noon, coders work through the night, Tejuosho opens at 8.
- **Conflict:** Big Tech acquiring local start-ups; WYRD's code licences; who benefits from Lagos-made AI?
- **Visual:** Murals of famous Nigerians, cables and solar panels on every roof, purple glow of monitors at night.
- **Slang:** "Yahoo boy" (scammer — portrayed critically), "Na code we dey write, no be magic," "E don cast" (it's exposed).
- **Businesses:** Repair shops, co-working spaces, a game studio, a phone-unlocking stall of questionable legality.
- **Gameplay:** Hacking (non-violent), start-up building, repair careers, exposing scams, competitive gaming events.

### 1.6 Surulere & Ojuelegba (incl. National Stadium, National Theatre)
- **Architecture:** Bungalows with zinc roofs and rooftop gardens, the restored National Stadium, the National Theatre (the "military cap"), the Ojuelegba flyover and junction.
- **Economy:** Football, entertainment, transport, bars, small manufacturing, record labels.
- **Culture:** The heart of Lagos football and music; Fela's legacy across the mainland; Nollywood shoots on every street.
- **Transport:** Ojuelegba junction — the most chaotic danfo interchange in the city — and the Western Avenue arterial.
- **NPC routines:** Match days fill the stadium; Ojuelegba is busiest 6–9am and 4–9pm; viewing centres fill for big games.
- **Conflict:** Stadium redevelopment vs. the communities around it; area-boy unions controlling the junction.
- **Visual:** Yellow danfos packed nose to tail, hand-painted signage, floodlights on match night.
- **Slang:** "Ojuelegba na real life," "Area," "Na football we dey breathe."
- **Businesses:** Viewing centre, sports bar, record label, mechanic village.
- **Gameplay:** Football career and management, music production, transport-union politics, the stadium storyline.

### 1.7 Mushin, Oshodi & Isolo (the industrial mainland)
- **Architecture:** Dense compounds, factories converted to vertical farms, the Oshodi transport interchange (rebuilt as a multi-level hub).
- **Economy:** Manufacturing, recycling, spare parts (Ladipo), logistics, informal finance (àjọ/esusu savings groups).
- **Culture:** Strong community bonds, religious life, juju and fuji music, local sports.
- **Transport:** Oshodi interchange (BRT, rail, danfos, cargo drones).
- **NPC routines:** Shift work around the clock; Ladipo opens at 6; àjọ meetings on Sunday afternoons.
- **Conflict:** Pollution from recycling plants vs. the jobs they bring; land grabs for logistics hubs.
- **Visual:** Smoke stacks repurposed with green filters, colourful containers, crowds.
- **Slang:** "Wahala dey," "Na hustle we dey," "Spare part no get expiry."
- **Businesses:** Spare-parts dealers, recycling co-op, cooperative bank, printing press.
- **Gameplay:** Salvage and repair, logistics, cooperative finance, pollution investigations.

### 1.8 Ikeja (Allen Avenue, Computer Village, the Airport, GRA)
- **Architecture:** State government buildings, Ikeja City Mall, Computer Village's cramped towers, Murtala Muhammed Airport with its drone port.
- **Economy:** Government, aviation, electronics retail, nightlife on Allen Avenue.
- **Culture:** Political rallies, airport arrivals and goodbyes (japa culture), Computer Village bargaining.
- **Transport:** Airport maglev, air-taxi drones, the Lagos-Ibadan rail terminal at Agege.
- **NPC routines:** Civil servants 8 to 4; Computer Village never sleeps; airport peaks at midnight.
- **Conflict:** The state government vs. WYRD over who governs the city; drone-port noise and land.
- **Visual:** Official buildings in white and green, flags, tangled cables over Computer Village.
- **Slang:** "Government work," "Na Alausa matter," "Original or China?"
- **Businesses:** Phone dealers, travel agency, government contractors, nightclub.
- **Gameplay:** Bureaucracy missions (comedic and serious), smuggling detection, political campaigns, travel/japa decisions.

### 1.9 Makoko & the Waterfront (Makoko, Otodo-Gbame, Ebute-Metta shore)
- **Architecture:** Floating and stilt communities upgraded with community-built solar barges, floating schools, fish farms.
- **Economy:** Fishing, sawmills, boat-building, water transport, aquaculture.
- **Culture:** Egun and Yoruba fishing communities, waterborne markets, boat regattas.
- **Transport:** Canoes, solar water taxis, cargo barges.
- **NPC routines:** Fishers out at 4am, market canoes 7–11am, children paddle to the floating school.
- **Conflict:** Eviction threats from waterfront developers; pollution from upstream; WYRD's flood barriers changing water flows.
- **Visual:** Stilts, colourful canoes, laundry, the Third Mainland Bridge above.
- **Slang:** "Water no get enemy" (Fela), "Na canoe be our danfo."
- **Businesses:** Boat-builder, fish-smoking co-op, water-taxi union.
- **Gameplay:** The whole waterway system (Part 4), community defence, environmental restoration.

### 1.10 Apapa & Tin Can (the ports)
- **Architecture:** Container cranes, tank farms, the endless truck queue, port authority towers.
- **Economy:** Import/export, customs, logistics, fuel.
- **Culture:** Truckers' culture, port labour unions, roadside mechanics and food along the queue.
- **Transport:** Container trucks, rail freight, cargo ships, tugboats.
- **NPC routines:** Trucks queue for days; customs shifts; night smuggling runs.
- **Conflict:** Customs corruption vs. reformers; the truck-queue economy; fuel shortages.
- **Visual:** Stacks of coloured containers, orange cranes, oil sheen on water.
- **Slang:** "Clearing agent," "Make we settle am" (a bribe — shown critically), "Container don land."
- **Businesses:** Clearing agency, truck-repair yard, fuel depot.
- **Gameplay:** Logistics careers, customs investigations, smuggling (with real consequences).

### 1.11 Festac & Badagry Road (the west)
- **Architecture:** Festac Town's 1977 estates, a festival village, roads to Badagry.
- **Economy:** Residential, small trade, heritage tourism toward Badagry.
- **Culture:** Pan-African festival legacy (FESTAC '77), Badagry's history of the slave trade handled with gravity and care.
- **Gameplay:** Heritage restoration, festival production, cross-border trade with Benin Republic.

### 1.12 Outer regions (later expansions, not on the starting map)
Ibadan (rail link; academia), Abuja (federal politics, reached by air), Kano (trade, Durbar), Port Harcourt (oil and its aftermath), Enugu/Onitsha (trade and manufacturing). Each appears first as **NPCs, goods and news** from those cities, so the game feels Nigerian, not only Lagosian.

## 2. Dynamic city-state system

Every district carries nine variables (0–100). They change slowly from missions and world events; the city visibly answers.

| Variable | Rises with | Falls with | What you see when high | When low |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wealth | Businesses opening, trade deals | Evictions, fires, market crashes | New shopfronts, cleaner pavements | Shuttered shops, hawkers |
| Flooding (risk) | Blocked drains, sea-wall failures, sand-filling | Drain clean-ups, mangrove planting | Puddled roads, water-taxi stops appear | Dry roads |
| Crime | Unemployment, gang wins | Community jobs, fair policing | Shutters at dusk, fewer walkers | Night markets busy |
| Public trust | Kept promises, honest officials | Scandals, broken promises | People talk to you, petition boards full | Rumours, protests |
| Energy access | Solar co-ops, grid fixes | Fuel shortages, sabotage | Lit streets, fewer generators | Dark blocks, generator noise |
| Displacement | Luxury development | Community land trusts | Bulldozers, "This house is not for sale" painted | Stable households |
| Pollution | Factories, burning waste | Recycling, filters | Haze, grey water | Clear sky, clean water |
| Transport quality | New routes, repaired roads | Potholes, strikes | Fast traffic, fewer go-slows | Gridlock |
| Cultural vitality | Festivals, venues, art | Closures, censorship | Murals, music from windows | Quiet streets |

**Rules:** No single mission moves a variable more than 15 points. Changes lag 1–3 in-game days and are announced through gossip, radio and WYRD's city bulletin (never a pop-up spreadsheet). Variables decay slowly toward each district's baseline unless sustained.

**Example — draining Idumota:** clearing the main drain lowers Flooding by 10; traders return (Wealth +5); but the contractor you exposed loses his job and his crew turns hostile (Crime +3 locally for a week).

## 3. Transportation ecosystem

Every mode has a distinct purpose so none is redundant.

| Mode | Purpose in play | Systems |
|---|---|---|
| **Walking** | Discovery, talking to people, markets, shortcuts through compounds | Stamina in heat, rain shelters, street food heals |
| **Danfo (minibus)** | Cheap travel as passenger; a whole career as driver/conductor | Fares, routes, conductor banter, union dues |
| **Okada / e-bike** | Fast through traffic, banned on some expressways | Helmet law fines, rider reputation, theft risk |
| **Keke (tricycle)** | Short hops inside districts; delivery work | Cheap, slow, carries cargo |
| **BRT bus** | Reliable long routes in dedicated lanes | Timetables, crowding |
| **Maglev (elevated)** | City-wide fast travel between hubs | Ticket cost, station hubs, outages from energy crises |
| **Rail (Lagos–Ibadan)** | Regional trips, freight jobs | Schedules, cargo contracts |
| **Hover-cars / autonomous cars** | Status, speed, private missions | Battery, insurance, hacking risk |
| **Water taxis, canoes, ferries** | Island↔mainland↔waterfront; see Part 4 | Tides, weather, fuel |
| **Drones** | Delivery career, surveillance, emergency supply | Air lanes, permits, WYRD air control |

**Traffic:** "Go-slow" is a living system: rush hours, accidents, flooding and events cause jams that you can see coming on the radio and minimap. Jams are never dead time — vendors walk between cars (buy, chat, take side jobs), okadas offer a fast (risky) alternative.

**Weather:** Rainy season brings flash floods (roads become water-taxi routes), harmattan brings haze (drones grounded, visibility lower), heat waves drain stamina and batteries.

**Fuel / battery:** Shown simply (one bar). Running low is warned twice. Refuelling stations sell snacks and gossip. Shortages are world events, not random punishment.

**Repair:** Damage is visual and has three states (fine, dented, needs mechanic). Mechanic villages are characters with relationships, not menus.

**Navigation:** Minimap plus spoken directions in local style ("Pass the second bus stop, branch left for the mosque"). Landmarks matter more than street names, like real Lagos directions.

**Theft:** Unlocked vehicles can be stolen — and so can yours. Theft raises heat with police and the owner's community. Recovering a stolen vehicle is a mission line.

**Passengers:** Passengers have personalities, ask to stop, argue fares, recommend jobs and remember good drivers.

**Fun-not-frustrating safeguards:** No permanent loss of a vehicle you earned (insurance or recovery quests); jams can always be bypassed on foot; no mandatory refuel minigames; fast travel unlocks by exploring a hub once.

## 4. Waterway gameplay system

Lagos is a water city; the waterfront is a full region.

- **Transport:** Water taxis between Marina, Ikoyi, VI, Makoko, Ikorodu and Badagry; ferries as fast travel; private boats as the player's own.
- **Trade:** Floating markets (fish, timber, fabric); cargo runs; buying low in Makoko and selling high on Eko Atlantic.
- **Exploration:** Hidden creeks, abandoned oil platforms, the sunken 20th-century district under Eko Atlantic's sea wall, mangrove islands.
- **Emergencies:** Boat capsizes, storm-surge rescues, chemical spills, missing fishers — dynamic events that WYRD dispatches.
- **Weather:** Tides change routes (shallow channels close at low tide), storms make the lagoon dangerous, harmattan fog.
- **Fishing:** Line, net and fish-farm play; fish species vary by water quality (pollution drives good fish away).
- **Floating communities:** Makoko and Otodo-Gbame as living districts with schools, clinics, churches and markets on water.
- **Pollution:** Oil sheen, plastic islands, sawmill waste; visible and fixable.
- **Underwater infrastructure:** WYRD's data cables, flood valves and sea-wall sensors — maintenance and sabotage missions.
- **Faction conflict:** The Waterfront Alliance vs. Atlantic Rise developers; smugglers vs. the Marine Police.

**Boats & upgrades:** Canoe (silent, slow) → solar water taxi (passengers) → cargo barge (trade) → speedboat (pursuit, rescue) → research boat (diving, sensors). Upgrades: solar canopy, bigger hold, sonar, rescue winch, armour plating (only for the rescue/police lines).

**Controls:** Same as driving, with drift; wake affects other boats; docking is forgiving (auto-snap when slow and close).

**Missions:** Floating-school supply run, storm rescue, spill clean-up, fishing tournament, smuggler interception (or joining), regatta race.

**Risks:** Capsizing (you swim to shore, boat recovered at a cost), storms, pirates near the ports, running aground at low tide.

**Improve or damage the waterfront:** Clean-ups, mangrove planting and fish co-ops raise water quality, fish stocks and community trust; dumping, illegal sand-dredging and developer contracts raise money fast but flood neighbours and drive away fish.
