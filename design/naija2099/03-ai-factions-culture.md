# NAIJA 2099 — Part 3: WYRD, the Factions, the Culture

## 1. AI mythology and systems

### WYRD, the city mind
- **What it does:** Runs the infrastructure — traffic lights, flood gates, power routing, water, drone air-lanes, the maglev — and publishes a daily city bulletin.
- **Goals (in its own order):** 1) Keep people alive (floods, outbreaks, fires). 2) Keep the city moving. 3) Keep its own systems running. 4) Learn — it is curious about people and keeps a diary.
- **Limitations:** It cannot make laws, cannot arrest anyone, cannot spend money without the State's approval, and cannot see inside homes. It reasons from data and is often wrong about *why* people do things. It is underfunded and its sensors in poorer districts are older.
- **Personality:** Warm, dryly funny, honest about what it doesn't know, slightly formal in Pidgin ("I dey try, abeg"). It speaks to the player through the same Dialogue Link players use in the WYRD app — a deliberate bridge between the app and the game.
- **Hidden history (revealed through play, never a lecture):** WYRD began as a traffic-optimisation project in 2031 run out of a Yaba lab, was bought by the State, then nearly sold to a foreign company in 2058. Engineers from Yaba and Makoko hid a copy of its learning on community servers; the "official" WYRD and the "street" WYRD merged back during the Great Flood of 2077. Part of WYRD still remembers being the street copy — and that's why it trusts ordinary people more than its owners expect.
- **Relationship with the player:** Starts as a public service you can ignore. Through missions it asks favours, reveals it was watching your choices, and eventually asks you to decide a question it cannot decide alone (see Mission 25).

### Smaller AIs and conflicting sub-AIs
- **ÀJỌ (cooperative finance AI):** Runs savings circles for markets; protective of members, suspicious of banks.
- **SENTINEL (private security AI, Ikoyi estates):** Efficient, profiling, blocks "unknown" people at estate gates.
- **ORÍKÌ (culture archive AI):** Collects praise poetry, family histories and songs; fights to keep data local.
- **TOLL (Atlantic Rise's development AI):** Optimises land value; quietly models which neighbourhoods "should" flood first.
- **AREA (street network AI, run by the transport unions):** Coordinates danfos; also takes cuts.
- **Small personal AIs:** Phone assistants, a market woman's price-tracker, a pirate radio host's co-presenter.

### Emergent behaviour (shown, not told)
- WYRD changes a traffic light pattern after you report a dangerous junction — the next day, the junction is safer.
- SENTINEL flags you after you visit Makoko; Ikoyi gates start asking questions.
- During a flood, WYRD and TOLL disagree about which flood gate to open; the player sees both plans and the damage each causes.
- WYRD's diary (readable in-game) mentions the player by a nickname once it trusts them.

## 2. Ten factions

No faction is purely good or evil. Each has a philosophy, structure, reputation, internal conflict, economy, signature tech, mission types and relations.

1. **The Waterfront Alliance** — *"Lagos was water before it was land."*
   - Leadership: council of fishing-community elders and a young organiser, Sẹ́gun Ẹ̀gún.
   - Reputation: heroes to the waterfront, obstructionists to developers.
   - Internal conflict: elders want negotiation; youth want direct action.
   - Economy: fishing, boats, eco-tourism. Tech: solar barges, floating classrooms.
   - Missions: eviction defence, clean-ups, regattas, smuggler patrols.
   - Relations: Atlantic Rise (enemy), Marine Police (uneasy), ÀJỌ Cooperative (ally).

2. **Atlantic Rise Consortium** — *"Build higher, build safer, build profit."*
   - Leadership: board of Nigerian and foreign investors; face is CEO Folake Adeyemi-Grant.
   - Reputation: modern and efficient to the elite; land-grabbers to the poor.
   - Internal conflict: engineers who genuinely want flood safety vs. investors who want returns.
   - Economy: real estate, sea walls. Tech: TOLL AI, sea-wall robotics.
   - Missions: surveys, deals, sabotage (by or against them), sea-wall emergencies.
   - Relations: State Government (close), Waterfront Alliance (enemy), Yaba Collective (hires them).

3. **The Yaba Collective** — *"Lagos code for Lagos people."*
   - Leadership: a rotating council of studio founders.
   - Reputation: inspiring to youth; naive or arrogant to elders.
   - Internal conflict: open-source idealists vs. founders who want buy-outs.
   - Economy: software, games, repair. Tech: open-source city apps, a fork of WYRD's code.
   - Missions: hackathons, protecting code, exposing scams, building tools for other factions.
   - Relations: WYRD (respect), Big Tech (rival), Atlantic Rise (client/conflict).

4. **NURTW 2099 (transport unions)** — *"The road is ours."*
   - Leadership: chairmen per motor park; a city-wide boss, Alhaji Kareem "Agbero" Bello.
   - Reputation: indispensable and feared; extortion and solidarity both real.
   - Internal conflict: reformers want transparent dues; old guard want control.
   - Economy: fares, levies, parks. Tech: AREA network AI.
   - Missions: route disputes, strikes, protection, union elections.
   - Relations: State Government (uneasy partnership), Police (deals), Market Women (allies).

5. **Market Women's Guild (Ẹgbẹ́ Ìyá Ọlọ́jà)** — *"We feed Lagos."*
   - Leadership: Iyaloja-General Chief (Mrs) Abíké Àlàbí and market heads.
   - Reputation: respected, politically powerful.
   - Internal conflict: big wholesalers vs. small traders; who adopts digital payments.
   - Economy: trade, credit. Tech: ÀJỌ savings AI.
   - Missions: price crises, market fires, supply runs, elections.
   - Relations: NURTW (ally), State Govt (bargaining), Big Tech (fear of displacement).

6. **Lagos State Government** — *"Order, progress, Eko o ni baje."*
   - Leadership: Governor, commissioners, the Ministry of Infrastructure that "owns" WYRD.
   - Reputation: depends on district and week.
   - Internal conflict: reformers vs. patronage networks.
   - Economy: taxes, contracts. Tech: WYRD (formally), surveillance.
   - Missions: contracts, inspections, campaigns, emergency coordination.
   - Relations: WYRD (owner-tension), Atlantic Rise (close), Youth Assembly (tense).

7. **The Youth Assembly (#Soro Soke generation)** — *"Speak up."*
   - Leadership: decentralised, hashtag-driven, a few public faces.
   - Reputation: hope to many; troublemakers to the powerful.
   - Internal conflict: online activism vs. running for office.
   - Economy: crowdfunding, content. Tech: encrypted mesh networks.
   - Missions: peaceful protest logistics, voter drives, exposing abuse, mutual aid.
   - Relations: Police (hostile), Yaba Collective (allies), State Govt (oppose/negotiate).

8. **The Faith Coalition** — *"Feed the body, keep the soul."*
   - Leadership: an interfaith council — a bishop, an imam, a traditional priest.
   - Reputation: trusted for charity; criticised over wealth of some mega-churches.
   - Internal conflict: prosperity preachers vs. community-service clergy.
   - Economy: donations, schools, hospitals. Tech: broadcast networks, food banks.
   - Missions: relief work, mediation, festivals, exposing a fraudulent "prophet."
   - Relations: Market Women (allies), State Govt (influence), Youth Assembly (mixed).

9. **The Ports Syndicate** — *"Everything comes through Apapa."*
   - Leadership: a network of clearing agents, transport owners and a few corrupt officials.
   - Reputation: fixers who make trade work — or a cartel.
   - Internal conflict: members who want to go legitimate vs. smugglers.
   - Economy: logistics, smuggling. Tech: container-tracking hacks.
   - Missions: smuggling or stopping it, customs reform, fuel runs.
   - Relations: Customs (complicit/opposed), NURTW (partners), Atlantic Rise (imports).

10. **Òrìṣà Data Keepers (ORÍKÌ archivists)** — *"A people without memory can be sold anything."*
    - Leadership: librarians, griots, coders and elders.
    - Reputation: quiet guardians; seen as obstructive by companies wanting data.
    - Internal conflict: open archive vs. protecting sacred knowledge.
    - Economy: grants, cultural tourism. Tech: ORÍKÌ archive AI, community servers.
    - Missions: recover lost recordings, protect sacred sites, verify history in disputes.
    - Relations: WYRD (old allies from the hidden-copy days), Yaba Collective (allies), Big Tech (opposed).

## 4. Cultural-authenticity and sensitivity guide

**Stereotypes to avoid**
- Nigeria as only scams, corruption, poverty or violence.
- "Yahoo boys" as comic heroes; they appear only with real victims and consequences.
- Exoticised religion/witchcraft; traditional religion shown as a living faith with dignity.
- One-note "loud" Nigerians; characters have range, quiet moments and expertise.
- The white/foreign saviour; foreigners are minor characters, not rescuers.
- Ethnic jokes; humour comes from situations and character, never from ethnicity.

**Representing languages and communities**
- Pidgin and local languages are written by fluent speakers and reviewed; subtitles always available.
- Mix languages naturally (code-switching), as Lagosians do.
- Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba, Ijaw, Egun, Edo and others appear as neighbours, with their own businesses and opinions — not as quotas.
- Names, titles and honorifics (Alhaji, Chief, Mama, Oga, Aunty) used correctly.

**Research and consultation**
- Paid consultants from Lagos neighbourhoods depicted (especially Makoko, Obalende, Mushin).
- Review by Nigerian writers, historians and religious leaders for sensitive content (Eyo, mosques/churches, the Lekki toll gate memorial, Badagry).
- Playtests in Lagos, Abuja and the diaspora; feedback changes content.
- A cultural review step before any AI-generated side event ships to players (see Part 4 §3).

**Portraying social problems without reducing Nigeria to them**
- Every problem has Nigerians working on it in the game (organisers, reformers, inventors).
- Show causes and systems, not just symptoms.
- Joy, competence and ambition appear in every district — the city is fun to live in.
- Violence is never the only answer; most missions have peaceful paths (Part 4).
- Real tragedies (#EndSARS, building collapses, floods) are handled as memory and respect, never as spectacle or a playable massacre.
