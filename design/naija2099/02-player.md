# NAIJA 2099 — Part 2: The Player, the Hustle, the Money

## 1. Branching character system

Identity is built from seven strands. None locks a door forever; each changes which doors open first and who opens them.

| Strand | What it is | How it grows |
|---|---|---|
| **Background** | Where you start (six options below) | Chosen once |
| **Skills** | Driving, Talk, Trade, Tech, Craft, Street, Water, Care | Use-based: you get better at what you do |
| **Relationships** | People who know you (see §5) | Favours, promises, time spent |
| **Reputation** | Many separate reputations (see §4) | Actions, witnessed or reported |
| **Clothing** | Owambe, street, corporate, uniform, traditional | Changes how NPCs first read you; never a stat gate |
| **Business choices** | What you own and how you run it | Investment, staff treatment |
| **Moral decisions** | Recorded as *what you did*, not as a good/evil meter | Consequences through reputation and relationships |

### Six starting backgrounds

Each changes the first ten hours; by about hour 10–12 every career is reachable from every background.

**1. Danfo Conductor's Child (Ojuelegba)**
- Start: on the step of your uncle's danfo, shouting routes. Skills: Street, Talk. Contacts: transport union, mechanics, market women.
- First ten hours: you learn the city through routes; early income is fares and errands; the union's internal fight is your first big choice (back your uncle or the reformer).
- Never prevents: corporate or tech careers — Yaba's bootcamp recruits street-smart people; your route knowledge becomes a logistics start-up.

**2. Returnee from Abroad ("Japa reverse")**
- Start: landing at Murtala Muhammed Airport with savings in foreign currency and rusty Yoruba/Pidgin. Skills: Tech, Trade. Contacts: a cousin in Lekki, an old schoolmate in VI.
- First ten hours: culture-shock comedy (prices, bargaining, family expectations); people overcharge you until your Talk improves; family asks for "support."
- Never prevents: street careers — earning respect in markets is a questline, not a lock.

**3. Makoko Fisher's Daughter/Son**
- Start: on the water at 4am. Skills: Water, Craft. Contacts: the Waterfront Alliance, the floating-school teacher.
- First ten hours: waterway play first; eviction threat drives the opening arc; you see the city from below the bridge.
- Never prevents: land careers — selling fish leads to markets, then trade, then anything.

**4. Yaba Coding Dropout**
- Start: in a hostel with a broken laptop and a half-finished app. Skills: Tech, Street. Contacts: hackathon crew, a crypto hustler, a repair shop.
- First ten hours: repair and small hacks; a scam you almost fall into becomes a moral choice (expose it or profit).
- Never prevents: physical careers — your first delivery bike pays the rent.

**5. Ikoyi Heir with a Problem**
- Start: in a villa; the family business is under investigation. Skills: Talk, Trade. Contacts: lawyers, society aunties, a loyal driver.
- First ten hours: money is easy but trust is not; the driver and gateman become your guides to the "other" Lagos; the investigation forces choices about family loyalty.
- Never prevents: humble careers — losing the money is a possible path; so is keeping it ethically.

**6. Nurse from Ibadan, newly posted**
- Start: a night shift at a Surulere clinic. Skills: Care, Talk. Contacts: doctors, patients from every class, ambulance crew.
- First ten hours: emergencies across the city introduce districts fast; triage choices (who first?) shape early reputations.
- Never prevents: any career — patients become contacts in every field.

## 2. The hustle economy — careers

Fourteen careers; *(Q)* marks morally questionable ones. All share: start small, own equipment, build a crew, own a business.

| Career | Core loop | Equipment | Risks | Progression | NPC relationships | Income | Reputation effects | AI-generated twists |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Danfo driver** | Drive route, load at stops, manage fares | Danfo (rented → owned → fleet) | Breakdowns, union dues, police checkpoints | Route → own bus → fleet → union seat | Conductor, union boss, mechanics, regular passengers | ₦ per passenger; steady | Union and street rep up | A pregnant passenger in labour; a VIP hides from paparazzi in your bus |
| **Market trader** | Buy wholesale, price, haggle, sell | Stall, storage, a phone | Spoilage, price shocks, fire | Stall → shop → warehouse → import line | Market queen (Iyaloja), suppliers, rivals | Margin; seasonal | Market rep, class rep | A festival doubles pepper prices overnight; a supplier offers suspiciously cheap goods |
| **Delivery rider** | Collect, navigate, deliver on time | Bike → e-bike → drone | Theft, crashes, rain | Rider → own fleet → logistics app | Dispatchers, customers, other riders | Per job + tips | Speed and honesty rep | A parcel is evidence in a scandal; customer is a celebrity |
| **Mechanic / repair** | Diagnose, fix, upsell parts | Tools, parts, workshop | Fake parts, unpaid bills | Roadside → workshop → mechanic village boss | Spare-part dealers, drivers | Per job | Craft rep; trust | A hover-car with a hacked autopilot arrives |
| **Software builder** | Take contracts, ship apps | Laptop, servers, team | Bugs, stolen code, Big Tech buy-outs | Freelancer → studio → company | Clients, coders, investors | Lumpy contracts | Tech rep | WYRD offers a city contract with strings attached |
| **Event producer (owambe)** | Plan, staff, run parties | Canopies, sound, drones, caterers | Rain, power cuts, a feuding family | Small parties → society weddings → concerts | Aunties, DJs, caterers, celebrities | Big per event | Society rep | Two families claim the same hall; the groom vanishes |
| **Musician / producer** | Write, record, perform, grow fans | Studio gear, a crew | Piracy, bad contracts | Street gigs → label → headline | Producers, promoters, fans | Royalties + shows | Online audience | A song goes viral for the wrong reason |
| **Fisher / aquaculture** | Fish, farm, sell at market | Canoe → boat → fish farm | Storms, pollution | Canoe → co-op → export | Fish smokers, market women | Daily catch | Waterfront rep | Rare fish appears after a clean-up |
| **Nurse / medic** | Respond, triage, treat | Kit, ambulance | Burnout, impossible choices | Clinic → ambulance → own clinic | Patients across classes, doctors | Salary + private work | Trust in every district | Outbreak event; a patient is a wanted person |
| **Journalist / content creator** | Investigate, publish, grow audience | Camera drone, phone | Lawsuits, threats | Blog → channel → newsroom | Sources, editors, officials | Ads + sponsors | Online audience; government suspicion | A leak lands in your inbox |
| **Clearing agent (ports)** *(Q)* | Move goods through customs | Licence, contacts | Corruption temptations, raids | Clerk → agency | Customs officers, importers | Fees (+ "settlement" if you choose) | Business vs. anti-corruption rep | A container you clear holds something illegal |
| **Fixer / middleman** *(Q)* | Connect people who need things | Phone, network | Betrayal, being used | Small favours → power broker | Everyone | Commissions | Mixed: useful but distrusted | A favour asked by WYRD itself |
| **Fuel arbitrage ("black market")** *(Q)* | Buy during plenty, sell in shortage | Storage, transport | Fires, police, community anger | Jerrycans → depot | Tanker drivers, desperate customers | Very high in crises | Profit vs. community rep | A shortage you could end by selling at cost |
| **Hacker for hire** *(Q)* | Break into systems for clients | Rig, exploits | Getting caught, hurting innocents | Script kid → elite | Clients from every faction | High per job | Faction-dependent | A contract would expose a hospital |

Every questionable career has an honest version reachable by the same skills (e.g. hacker → security consultant; fixer → community organiser).

## 3. Dynamic economy

- **Markets:** Each district market has prices for ~40 goods (food, fabric, electronics, parts, fuel, fish). Prices drift with supply, demand and events.
- **Transport costs:** Fares rise with fuel price and jams; your own transport costs battery/fuel and repairs.
- **Food:** Staples (rice, garri, yam, pepper, tomatoes) respond to rain, harvests and imports; a "tomato crisis" event is real Lagos.
- **Energy:** Grid availability per district; generators cost fuel; solar co-ops lower costs over time.
- **Housing:** Rent per district; can be raised by gentrification; land trusts cap it.
- **Technology:** Prices fall over time but import shocks spike them.
- **Labour:** Wages by profession; your staff expect fair pay and remember how you treat them.
- **Imports/exports:** Port events (strikes, raids, currency swings) ripple into prices city-wide within a day.
- **Player businesses:** Stall, shop, workshop, studio, fleet, restaurant, event company, fish farm. Each has staff, stock, upkeep, reputation.

**Price response examples:** a flood on Lekki-Epe raises fish prices in Lekki (+20%) and drops them in Makoko (gluts); your solar co-op in Mushin lowers generator fuel demand, dropping fuel prices nearby.

**Safeguards against tedium/unfairness:**
- Businesses run themselves when you're away (a manager handles stock); you step in for decisions, not chores.
- No price change bigger than ±30% without a visible, announced event.
- Shocks always have a counter-play (stockpile, substitute, help others).
- Bankruptcy is a story, not a game over: loans from the àjọ, family, or a rival with conditions.
- Daily summaries are short and in a character's voice (your shop manager texts you).

## 4. Reputation & consequence system

Reputation is never one number. Each track is −100 to +100, with memory of *why*.

- **Neighbourhood** (per district): do people there trust you?
- **Faction** (ten factions; Part 3)
- **Profession** (respect within your career's circle)
- **Social class** (street, middle, elite) — how each class reads you
- **Online audience** (followers, sentiment)
- **Family** (your own; see §5)
- **Government agencies:** Police, LASTMA (traffic), Customs, State Government, WYRD itself

**Witnesses:** Only witnessed or reported actions change reputation. Rumours spread through NPC gossip over time; a journalist can make something spread instantly.

**Examples of one action, several consequences:**
1. *You refuse to pay a checkpoint bribe and film it.* Online audience +15, Street +8, Police −20, LASTMA −5; your uncle (a driver) worries you'll bring trouble to the family (Family −3).
2. *You sell fuel at cost during a shortage.* Mushin +12, Online +6, Fuel cartel −15 (they send a warning), your own business cash −₦300k.
3. *You win an Ikoyi wedding contract by undercutting a market-women caterer.* Elite +8, Event producers +5, Market women −10, Iyaloja −8 (prices go up for you at Balogun).
4. *You expose a Yaba crypto scam.* Tech community +10, Online +12, the scam crew's faction −25, but two of your hostel friends lose money and blame you (Relationship −).
5. *You let a starving thief go.* Mushin +6, Police −5 (if witnessed), the thief returns later as an informant.
6. *You help WYRD reroute traffic through a poor district to clear a VIP convoy.* WYRD +10, State Govt +8, that district −15, Transport union −6.

## 5. Relationships & family

Characters remember (stored as short memory entries with dates):
- **Promises** (kept/broken, with the deadline)
- **Betrayals** (and who saw them)
- **Financial help** (loans given and owed; gifts)
- **Missed events** (birthdays, naming ceremonies, funerals, a friend's show)
- **Public behaviour** (what they saw or heard about you)
- **Faction affiliations** (they may dislike your friends)

**Family:** a mother, an uncle/aunt, siblings and cousins (varies by background). Family asks for money, invites you to events, introduces you to work, and holds you to promises. Ignoring family is allowed — but they'll stop introducing you to opportunities and the ending reflects it.

**Relationship arcs (examples):**
- **Aunty Bisi (market queen's deputy):** from suspicious → trading partner → co-owner of a warehouse. Breaking a price promise ends the arc and opens a rivalry line.
- **Chidi (Yaba coder friend):** from roommate → co-founder → either partner in a successful company or rival who sold out to Big Tech, depending on your choices about the WYRD contract.
- **Mama Tolu (your mother):** her health storyline; showing up (or not) at three key moments changes the epilogue.
- **Sergeant Ade (police):** an honest officer under pressure; help him and you gain a reliable ally; expose him wrongly and lose the only straight cop you know.
- **Romance:** optional, adult, respectful; partners have their own careers and opinions on your choices; breakups are possible and remembered.

**Unlocks:** relationships unlock missions (favours), businesses (partners invest), story branches (who stands with you in the finale) and endings (who is at your table in the closing scene).
